Philosophy

Weather HQ is built for people who want real weather data without the noise. The goal is a fast, honest dashboard: no ads, no tracking, no unnecessary complexity. Data is fetched fresh on every load from open, public APIs and never routed through a backend. It runs as a PWA so it installs on your phone and works offline with the last cached fetch — the right tool for a morning glance before you head out the door.

Forecast Models

The 10-day forecast runs two independent global models in parallel: the American GFS (NOAA's Global Forecast System) and ECMWF IFS (the European Centre's Integrated Forecast System, widely regarded as the gold standard for medium-range prediction). Both are fetched fresh via Open-Meteo on every load. When the two models agree on a high or low within 3°F, the dashboard shows a single temperature — the most likely outcome. When they diverge by more than 3°F, the forecast card shows a range instead, for example 67–72°, because showing false precision would be less honest than showing the disagreement. This matters most beyond day 5, where small differences in initial conditions can compound into larger divergence. Tapping a temperature range reveals the individual model readings so you can see which model is the outlier and weigh them yourself.

Nowcast

Nowcast is a live conditions layer that runs on top of the forecast. It fuses four independent data sources — weather radar, nearby station observations, real-time lightning strikes from the Blitzortung network, and model output — to answer what is happening at your exact location right now and how it will change over the next hour. Radar is the primary authority for precipitation, station reports fill in details radar can miss (like drizzle or fog), lightning strikes drive all thunderstorm classifications, and the model acts as a last-resort gap-fill when observational data is unavailable.

Hysteresis and damping are built into every layer so the display stays stable — rain doesn't flicker on and off when a cell briefly weakens, and the condition label won't bounce between states on borderline readings. Polling speeds up automatically when weather is active and slows down when skies are clear.

Alerts

Weather alerts are displayed in a three-lane hazard panel at the top of the dashboard, color-coded by source and severity.

Red lane — NWS alerts

Active NWS weather alerts for your location (US only). Alerts are classified into four severity tiers: advisory, watch, warning, and critical. The lane displays the highest-priority alert with its expiration time. Critical events trigger a flashing animation to draw attention. Tapping the lane opens a full modal listing every active and upcoming alert with headlines, descriptions, and recommended actions.

Yellow lane — SPC convective outlook

The Storm Prediction Center Day 1 categorical convective outlook for your location (US only). Categories range from Marginal (isolated severe storms possible) through High (major outbreak expected), each with a distinct color. The outlook is determined by a point-in-polygon match against the SPC GeoJSON polygons, so it reflects the specific risk zone your coordinates fall in rather than a broad regional summary.

Blue lane — near-term storms

A live summary of storm conditions derived from the nowcast system. This lane reflects the current lightning proximity state and precipitation status: whether lightning is active, nearby, or approaching, and whether rain is ongoing or imminent. It updates automatically as nowcast polls new radar and lightning data.

For non-US locations, the red and yellow lanes are hidden since NWS and SPC are US-only services. The blue lane works worldwide using RainViewer radar and the global Blitzortung lightning network.

Data Sources

  • Open-Meteo Forecast, current conditions, and multi-model comparison — free, open-source NWP API open-meteo.com ↗
  • NWS / NOAA Official US forecasts and active weather alerts (US locations only) api.weather.gov ↗
  • SPC / NOAA Storm Prediction Center Day 1 convective outlook — categorical severe weather risk (US only) spc.noaa.gov ↗
  • Meteostat Observed station data for recent precipitation history meteostat.net ↗
  • Open-Meteo AQI Air quality index derived from the European CAMS forecast model open-meteo.com/air-quality ↗
  • IEM NEXRAD US radar composite tiles at 5-min intervals — used by Nowcast for point sampling (US only) mesonet.agron.iastate.edu ↗
  • RainViewer Worldwide radar tiles — used by Nowcast for point sampling outside the US rainviewer.com ↗
  • Blitzortung Global real-time lightning detection via volunteer sensor network — live WebSocket feed used for strike distance classification and thunderstorm state blitzortung.org ↗

Architecture

Stack Vanilla JS · ES modules · no frameworks · no build step
Structure services/ fetch + normalize · ui/ render DOM · utils/ pure helpers · state/ single store
PWA Versioned service worker cache · installable on iOS & Android · offline serves last fetch
Data flow API → service normalizes → main.js orchestrates → renderer writes DOM once per refresh
